# Watchful waiting — when monitoring is the plan

Not every brain tumor is treated immediately. For some tumors,
particularly small or slow-growing ones found incidentally or with no
symptoms, the appropriate plan is **active surveillance** — sometimes
called "watch and wait" or "watchful waiting." This is not "doing
nothing." It is a structured plan with regular imaging, regular
check-ins, and clear triggers for changing course.

## Situations where it is often considered

- Some small **meningiomas** found by chance, growing slowly or not at
  all, especially in older adults.
- Some **low-grade gliomas** in carefully selected cases.
- Some **pituitary incidentalomas** — small pituitary lesions found on
  imaging done for unrelated reasons — that are not affecting hormones
  or vision.
- Other situations chosen by a multidisciplinary team after weighing the
  trade-offs.

## What "monitoring" actually looks like

- A baseline MRI, then follow-up scans at planned intervals — initially
  closer together (e.g., a few months), then spreading out as stability
  is confirmed.
- Specialist visits to discuss each scan and any new symptoms.
- A clear plan for what would change the approach: a defined growth
  rate, the appearance of certain features on imaging, new symptoms, or
  hormone changes (for pituitary cases).

## Why this is sometimes the better path

Treatment carries its own costs — surgical risk, radiation effects, time,
finances, side effects. When a tumor is unlikely to cause harm in a
foreseeable time frame, accepting the small risk of monitoring can be
better than the larger risk of intervening. The math of that trade-off
is highly individual.

## What watchful waiting is *not*

- Not "the doctors don't know what to do."
- Not a sign the situation is being taken less seriously.
- Not a permanent label — the plan can shift to active treatment if
  imaging or symptoms change.
