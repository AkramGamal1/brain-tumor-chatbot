# When to seek medical care

This chatbot is an educational tool. It cannot examine anyone, take a history,
or evaluate symptoms. The decision about *when* to seek care is one only a
person and the people around them can make — but here is general information
many medical sources point to.

## Reasons people commonly seek urgent or emergency care

If any of these are happening *right now*, do not wait for a chatbot. Call your
local emergency number (in the United States, 911) or go to an emergency
department:

- Sudden severe headache that is unlike previous headaches.
- Sudden weakness, numbness, or paralysis — especially on one side of the
  body, or in the face, arm, or leg.
- Sudden trouble speaking, understanding speech, or confusion.
- Sudden vision loss, double vision, or trouble seeing in one or both eyes.
- A first-ever seizure, or a seizure that lasts more than a few minutes, or
  multiple seizures in a row.
- Loss of consciousness or a head injury followed by confusion, vomiting, or
  worsening symptoms.
- Signs of a stroke — a useful summary: **F.A.S.T.** (Face drooping, Arm
  weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call emergency services).

## Reasons people commonly schedule a non-urgent appointment

These are things to bring to a primary-care doctor or specialist for an
evaluation, not necessarily an emergency:

- New, persistent headaches that are different from your usual pattern.
- Gradual changes in vision, hearing, or balance.
- Persistent nausea unexplained by another cause.
- Cognitive changes — trouble concentrating, memory changes, personality
  changes — that you or people close to you have noticed.
- Imaging results you don't fully understand and want explained.

## What this chatbot will *not* do

- Tell anyone whether their symptoms are an emergency. That requires
  examination and clinical judgment.
- Recommend specific tests, scans, or referrals.
- Give a second opinion on imaging.

If you are unsure, the safer choice is to call a clinician or an after-hours
nurse line and describe what is happening. They can triage in ways a chatbot
cannot.

This is not medical advice. Please consult a qualified clinician for any
medical decisions.
