# What is a pituitary tumor?

A **pituitary tumor** is a growth on the *pituitary gland* — a small,
pea-sized gland at the base of the brain that releases hormones controlling
many of the body's basic functions (growth, metabolism, stress response,
fertility, and others).

## Plain-language summary

- Most pituitary tumors are *adenomas*, which are typically benign
  (non-cancerous) growths.
- Pituitary tumors can affect the body in two main ways: by **producing extra
  hormones** (changing how the body works) or by **pressing on nearby
  structures**, including the nerves involved in vision.
- Some are very small and never cause symptoms; others need active treatment.
  The right next step depends on size, hormone activity, symptoms, and the
  person's overall health.

## Where the pituitary gland sits

The pituitary sits at the base of the brain, in a small bony pocket. Because
it is so close to the optic nerves, vision changes can sometimes be one of the
first noticed signs of a larger pituitary tumor — but most vision changes have
nothing to do with pituitary tumors and have many other causes.

## What this page does *not* tell you

- Whether anyone in particular has a pituitary tumor. The model looks at
  images; it does not diagnose people.
- Whether a tumor is hormone-producing, what hormone is involved, or how
  active it is.
- Whether watchful waiting, medication, surgery, or radiation is the right
  approach for any specific case.
- The outlook for any individual person.

A clinical team — typically including an endocrinologist and possibly a
neurosurgeon — is the right source for those answers.
