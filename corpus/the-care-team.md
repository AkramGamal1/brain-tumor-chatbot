# The care team — who does what

Brain-tumor care almost always involves a team of specialists, not one
doctor. Knowing who does what helps you understand who to ask which
question, and why several people may be involved in your case.

## The core specialists

- **Neurologist.** A doctor specialized in the brain and nervous system.
  Often involved in symptom management (seizures, headaches, cognition)
  and in coordinating evaluation, but does not typically perform surgery.
- **Neurosurgeon.** A surgeon specialized in the brain and spine.
  Performs biopsies, resections, and other surgical procedures.
- **Neuro-oncologist.** A medical doctor who specializes in tumors of the
  nervous system, particularly when chemotherapy or other systemic
  therapies are involved.
- **Radiation oncologist.** Plans and oversees radiation therapy when it
  is part of the plan.
- **Radiologist.** Reads the imaging and writes the report. Usually you
  do not meet them; their report goes to your other doctors.
- **Neuropathologist.** Examines biopsy tissue under the microscope and
  determines the tumor's type, grade, and molecular features.

## People who support the medical care

- **Oncology nurse navigator** or **nurse coordinator.** Often the most
  reachable person on the team day to day. Schedules, answers logistical
  questions, follows up between appointments.
- **Oncology social worker.** Helps with mental-health resources,
  financial concerns, transportation, family conversations.
- **Rehabilitation specialists** — physical, occupational, and speech
  therapists — if the tumor or treatment affects movement, language, or
  daily-living skills.
- **Palliative-care specialist.** Despite the name, palliative care is
  not only for end of life. They specialize in symptom relief and quality
  of life and can be involved at any stage.

## How decisions are made

Many centers run a regular **multidisciplinary team meeting** ("MDT" or
"tumor board") where the specialists review difficult cases together. The
plan you are offered usually reflects that group's recommendation, not
one person's view.
