# Common emotional responses to medical news

When someone receives news about a possible brain tumor, a wide range of
emotional responses can show up — sometimes one after another, sometimes
all at once. Knowing the common patterns can make them feel less
disorienting. None of these are signs that you are "handling it wrong."

## Patterns people often describe

- **Shock and disbelief.** "This isn't happening." A protective freeze
  that gives the mind time to catch up.
- **Fear**, often focused on specific worries: pain, loss of independence,
  effects on family, the unknown.
- **Anger**, sometimes directed at no one in particular, sometimes at
  doctors, family, or yourself. Anger is grief looking for somewhere to
  land.
- **Bargaining and "what ifs"** — replaying past habits or events as if
  they could explain the result. They almost never do.
- **Sadness and grief**, even before any clear news, for the version of
  the future you were imagining.
- **Acceptance is not a single moment.** People move through these states
  in loops, not in a straight line.

## Reactions in family and friends

The people around you will have their own version of these feelings.
Sometimes they will say the wrong thing because they are scared too. It
is OK to tell them what you need — even if what you need is space.

## When a reaction becomes something more

Most of these feelings ease over weeks as the situation becomes clearer
and a plan takes shape. Some signs it is worth talking to a clinician or
therapist:

- Daily life is significantly disrupted (sleep, eating, work, relationships).
- Feelings of hopelessness, panic, or numbness are constant rather than
  passing.
- Substance use is increasing as a way to cope.
- Thoughts of self-harm or suicide appear (please see
  `crisis-resources.md` immediately).
