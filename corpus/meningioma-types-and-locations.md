# Meningioma — types, locations, and what they mean

Meningiomas arise from the **meninges** — the protective layers around
the brain and spinal cord. They are one of the most common primary brain
tumors found in adults, and the majority are **benign** (not cancer)
and slow-growing. The plan for any specific meningioma depends heavily on
its location and how it is behaving.

## Grades, in plain terms

The World Health Organization classifies meningiomas into three grades:

- **Grade 1 (typical)**: the most common form, generally slow-growing,
  often able to be observed or surgically removed completely.
- **Grade 2 (atypical)**: a smaller fraction. More likely to grow back
  after surgery and may need radiation as well.
- **Grade 3 (anaplastic / malignant)**: rare, more aggressive, usually
  treated with a combination of surgery and radiation.

## Why location often matters as much as grade

Meningiomas can grow almost anywhere in the meninges. Common locations
include:

- **Convexity** (along the surface of the brain): often more
  straightforward to access surgically.
- **Parasagittal / falx** (along the midline): close to important
  draining veins, which affects the surgical approach.
- **Skull base** (under the brain): can sit near nerves and blood
  vessels controlling vision, hearing, balance, and facial movement.
- **Spinal**: arising in the spinal canal, with their own clinical
  picture.

A small meningioma in a "quiet" location may be watched indefinitely. A
similar-sized meningioma pressing on the optic nerve, the brainstem, or
a major venous structure may need treatment sooner.

## Common management options

- **Active surveillance** with periodic MRIs, especially for small,
  asymptomatic tumors.
- **Surgery**, with the goal of complete removal where safe.
- **Radiation therapy** — including stereotactic radiosurgery for some
  small tumors — when surgery is not the right tool.
- A combination, depending on grade, location, and what was achieved
  surgically.

## What this page is not

- Not a guess about *your* meningioma's grade or location.
- Not a prediction of what your team will recommend.
