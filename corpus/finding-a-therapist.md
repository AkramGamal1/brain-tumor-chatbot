# Finding a therapist

A therapist or counselor is a trained professional who can help you talk
through what you are feeling, build coping skills, and notice patterns you
might not see on your own. Many people find this useful at some point
during a medical journey, even those who have never sought therapy before.
You do not need to be "in crisis" to benefit from talking to one.

## Kinds of professionals who can help

- **Licensed therapist or counselor** (LCSW, LPC, MFT, psychologist).
  Trained in talk therapy. They do not prescribe medication.
- **Psychiatrist**. A medical doctor who can both talk with you and
  prescribe medication if it would help.
- **Psycho-oncology specialist**. A therapist who focuses specifically on
  people coping with serious medical illness. Many cancer centers have
  one on staff.
- **Hospital social worker**. Often available at no cost through the
  treating hospital; can connect you to local resources, support groups,
  and financial help.

## Practical ways to find one

- Ask the hospital or clinic where the imaging was done. Many have a
  social worker or patient navigator whose job is exactly this.
- Use a therapist directory: Psychology Today, the American Psychological
  Association locator, or your country's equivalent.
- Check whether your health insurance has an in-network mental-health
  directory — this can lower the cost.
- Ask a primary care doctor for a referral.

## What to expect from a first session

It is mostly the therapist asking what brought you in and listening. You
do not have to have it all figured out. It is normal to "try out" two or
three therapists before settling — fit matters as much as credentials.

## Where to read more

- Psychology Today therapist finder: https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/therapists
- American Psychological Association locator: https://locator.apa.org/
- CancerCare (free counseling for people affected by cancer): https://www.cancercare.org/

This is general information about finding mental-health support and is not
a recommendation that you specifically need therapy. This is not medical
advice. Please consult a qualified clinician for any medical decisions.
