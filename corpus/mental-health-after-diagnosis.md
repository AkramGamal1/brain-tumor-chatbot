# Mental health after a brain-imaging result

A brain-imaging result that mentions a tumor — or even a possible tumor —
is one of the most stressful pieces of news a person can receive. It is
common to experience anxiety, depressed mood, sleep disruption, intrusive
thoughts, or numbness in the days and weeks afterward. None of these
reactions are character flaws or signs of weakness; they are the mind and
body responding to a real shock.

## Reactions that often show up

- **Anxiety**: racing thoughts, restlessness, a constant background hum of
  worry, sometimes panicky moments.
- **Low mood**: loss of interest in things, heavy fatigue beyond what the
  body explains, tearfulness, difficulty concentrating.
- **Sleep changes**: trouble falling asleep, waking through the night, or
  sleeping much more than usual.
- **Hyper-vigilance about the body**: noticing every headache, twinge, or
  forgetful moment and wondering if it means something.
- **Disconnection**: feeling far away from yourself or from people you love.

These responses can be *information*. They tell you what kind of support
to look for.

## When it is worth telling someone

If any of the following last more than two weeks, are getting worse, or are
making daily life hard, please reach out to a clinician, therapist, or
trusted person:

- Persistent low mood, hopelessness, or loss of interest.
- Anxiety that does not ease or that is interfering with eating, sleeping,
  or working.
- Thoughts of harming yourself (see `crisis-resources.md`).
- Mood changes that feel "different" from your normal worry — particularly
  if loved ones notice them too. Mood symptoms can also reflect the
  underlying medical picture and are part of what a clinician should know.
