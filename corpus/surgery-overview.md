# Brain surgery — a layperson overview

Surgery for a brain tumor is performed by a **neurosurgeon**. The goal
varies by case: sometimes to remove as much tumor as is safe, sometimes
to take a tissue sample (biopsy), sometimes to relieve pressure or
manage symptoms. There is no single "brain tumor surgery" — there is a
family of procedures, each chosen for a specific situation.

## Common procedures

- **Craniotomy.** A section of skull is temporarily removed so the
  surgeon can access the brain, perform the procedure, and replace the
  bone afterward. This is the typical approach for tumor removal.
- **Stereotactic needle biopsy.** A small hole and a thin needle, guided
  by imaging, used to take a sample without a full craniotomy.
- **Awake craniotomy.** For tumors near areas controlling speech or
  movement, the patient is awakened during part of the procedure so the
  team can map function in real time and avoid damaging it.
- **Endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery.** Used for many pituitary
  tumors — the surgeon reaches the tumor through the nose and sphenoid
  sinus, avoiding a craniotomy entirely.

## What goes into the planning

Surgeons consider the tumor's location, size, type, the patient's overall
health, and what other treatments are likely to follow. A good plan is
about *which procedure, in what sequence, with what trade-offs* — not just
"yes or no, surgery."

## What recovery looks like in general

- A short hospital stay (often a few days for a straightforward
  craniotomy; longer for some cases).
- Fatigue lasting weeks to months. This is normal and not a sign of
  setback.
- Restrictions on driving, lifting, and flying for a defined period.
- Follow-up imaging to confirm what was achieved.

## What this page is not

- Not a recommendation that you have, or do not have, surgery. That is a
  conversation with a neurosurgeon who has reviewed your case.
- Not an estimate of any specific risk. Surgical risk is highly
  individual — location, function near the tumor, age, and other factors
  all matter.
