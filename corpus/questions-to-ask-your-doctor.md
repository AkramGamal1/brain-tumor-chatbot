# Questions to bring to your doctor

If imaging results have come back and an appointment is coming up, the time
in the room can feel short. Writing questions down beforehand helps.

These are common starting points. Skip anything that doesn't apply, and add
your own.

## About the imaging

- What did you see on the scan? Can you walk me through it?
- Are there things you're confident about, and things you're uncertain about?
- Does this image, on its own, tell you what's going on, or do you need more
  imaging or other tests?
- How does this compare to any earlier scans (if there are any)?
- Is the chatbot's interpretation consistent with yours? (If you used one.)

## About what's next

- What are the next steps you'd recommend, and why those?
- What other tests or specialists might be involved?
- How urgent is this — days, weeks, months?
- Are there things to watch for in the meantime that should make me call you?

## About options

- What are the possible treatment paths, and what are the trade-offs?
- What happens if we wait, watch, and re-image?
- What questions should I be asking that I haven't asked?
- Is a second opinion something you'd recommend or arrange?

## About living with this

- Are there activities I should change in the short term?
- How will this affect work, driving, or daily routine?
- What signs would mean things have gotten worse?
- Are there support resources — counselors, social workers, support groups —
  you can connect us with?

## Practical

- Can I have a written summary of today's visit?
- Who do I contact between visits if I have questions?
- What is the best way to share these results with other clinicians (primary
  care, others)?
- Are there costs or insurance questions I should sort out before the next
  step?

You can also ask: **"Is it okay if I record this conversation?"** Many
clinicians are fine with this and it lets you focus on listening rather than
note-taking.

This is not medical advice. Please consult a qualified clinician for any
medical decisions.
