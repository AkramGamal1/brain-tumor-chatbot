# Radiation therapy — a layperson overview

Radiation therapy uses high-energy beams to damage the DNA of tumor cells
so they cannot keep dividing. For brain tumors, radiation can be a primary
treatment, an addition to surgery, or a tool for symptom control. It is
overseen by a **radiation oncologist**.

## Common forms used for brain tumors

- **External-beam radiation therapy (EBRT).** Beams aimed from outside
  the body across multiple sessions ("fractions"), spread over weeks.
  This is the most common approach.
- **Stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS).** Despite the name, no incision —
  many beams converge on a precise target in one or a small number of
  sessions. Brand names include Gamma Knife and CyberKnife. Used for
  small, well-defined targets.
- **Stereotactic body radiotherapy / fractionated SRS.** The same precise
  targeting, given over a few sessions instead of one.
- **Proton-beam therapy.** Uses protons rather than X-rays; available at
  fewer centers. May be considered when sparing nearby healthy tissue is
  particularly important, especially in younger patients.

## What a course typically involves

- A planning visit, including a CT or MRI used to map the target and the
  surrounding structures.
- A custom mask molded to the head to keep position consistent across
  sessions.
- Daily sessions (each lasting minutes), usually weekdays, over a defined
  number of weeks. The radiation itself is painless during delivery.
- Regular check-ins with the radiation oncologist to manage side effects.

## Side-effect categories (general — not predictions)

- **Short-term**: fatigue, scalp irritation, hair loss in the treated
  area, mild nausea, headaches.
- **Later, after treatment ends**: changes in cognition, memory, or
  energy; rarely, more significant late effects depending on dose and
  area treated.
- **Pseudoprogression**, where post-radiation imaging looks worse before
  it looks better, can complicate interpretation of follow-up scans.

## What this page is not

- Not a recommendation about whether radiation is right for you.
- Not a prediction of your specific side effects, which depend on
  location, dose, technique, and you.
