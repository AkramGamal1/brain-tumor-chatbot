# The pituitary gland and what its tumors can affect

The **pituitary gland** is a small, pea-sized structure at the base of
the brain. Despite its size, it acts as a master regulator of many
hormone systems — controlling growth, metabolism, stress response,
fertility, and water balance through the hormones it produces and the
glands it directs.

## The hormone systems involved

Pituitary tumors can affect any of the following:

- **Growth hormone (GH)** — controls growth in children and metabolism
  in adults. Excess can cause **acromegaly** (in adults) or **gigantism**
  (in children); deficiency can cause low energy and metabolic effects.
- **Adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)** — drives cortisol release from
  the adrenal glands. Excess can cause **Cushing's disease**;
  deficiency is a serious medical issue requiring replacement.
- **Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)** — regulates the thyroid.
- **Prolactin** — supports breastfeeding; high levels can affect periods,
  fertility, and sometimes vision.
- **Luteinizing and follicle-stimulating hormones (LH, FSH)** — sex
  hormones and fertility.
- **Antidiuretic hormone (ADH)** and **oxytocin**, released from the back
  of the gland.

## Functioning vs. non-functioning tumors

- **Functioning** pituitary tumors over- or under-produce one or more
  hormones. The symptoms reflect which hormone is involved.
- **Non-functioning** pituitary tumors do not change hormone levels by
  themselves, but they can press on nearby structures — particularly the
  **optic chiasm** (causing visual field changes) or the rest of the
  pituitary (causing low hormone output as it is squeezed).

## How they are typically managed

- **Medications** that lower the offending hormone or shrink the tumor
  (for example, dopamine agonists for prolactinomas).
- **Endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery** — through the nose and sphenoid
  sinus — is the most common surgical approach.
- **Radiation therapy** when surgery is not enough or not feasible.
- **Hormone replacement** if the gland is under-producing.

## What this page is not

- Not a diagnosis of any specific person's pituitary status.
- Not a prediction about which symptom means which hormone.
